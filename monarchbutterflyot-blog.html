Why the Average Butterfly Doesn't Exist

Monarchs migrating from a dozen different fields don't converge because someone averaged their GPS coordinates. They converge because each one is following a curved corridor to the same destination. Drag the nodes below and watch the difference between averaging and actually aggregating.

Six migration routes, one overwintering site. No route is a straight line — and no two routes are the same shape.

Here is the question MonarchButterflyOT actually answers: when you have several distributions — policy checkpoints from different training nodes, in the case I care about — what does it mean to combine them into one?

The default answer in distributed training is: average the parameters. Add up the weight vectors, divide by the number of nodes, done. That operation is only correct if the space those parameters live in is flat — if straight-line interpolation between two points is actually the shortest, most meaningful path between them. Policy space, under the Wasserstein metric, is not flat. It's curved, the way the migratory corridors above are curved around geography that doesn't move in a straight line either.

The shaded region is a synthetic "obstacle" in policy space — a zone the true geometry routes around. The arithmetic mean walks straight through it anyway, because it doesn't know it's there. The Fréchet mean does.

At curvature zero, the two markers sit on top of each other — flat space, arithmetic averaging is fine. Push the slider up and they separate. That gap is not a rounding error. It's the entire reason a policy aggregated by naive averaging can under-perform every single one of the checkpoints that went into it: the average has been dragged into a region none of the original policies would have chosen to visit.

Averaging assumes the space doesn't push back. Wasserstein geometry is what happens once you admit that it does.

Treats every checkpoint as a point in flat Euclidean space. Fast, and wrong whenever the geometry is curved.

Where this connects

This is the same machinery behind WassersteinRL: once aggregation is framed as a Schrödinger bridge instead of a mean, the convergence guarantees hold under real heterogeneity — asynchronous nodes, different local data, different training speeds. MonarchButterflyOT is the geometric-aggregation half of that story, worked out on its own: Fréchet means, curved policy space, and exactly where arithmetic averaging quietly breaks.
